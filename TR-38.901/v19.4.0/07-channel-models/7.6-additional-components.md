# 7.6 Additional modelling components

Each one modifies specific steps of the 12-step generation procedure rather than replacing it, and each is applied only when the corresponding simulation feature is turned on. The TR is explicit that they are computationally more expensive and may not be required in all evaluation cases.

## 7.6.0 Introduction of additional modelling components

The components in this clause support advanced simulations: very large arrays and large bandwidth, oxygen absorption (53–67 GHz), spatial consistency (e.g. many closely located users), mobility, blockage, absolute time of arrival, dual mobility, sources of EM interference, devices embedded in machinery or enclosures, near-field effects (spherical wavefront), spatial non-stationarity (antenna element-wise power variation), number-of-cluster variability, and polarization power variability. They affect some of the steps between Step 1 and Step 12 of Clause 7.5.

## 7.6.1 Oxygen absorption

Oxygen absorption loss is applied to the cluster responses generated in **Step 11** of §7.5. The additional loss $OL_n(f_c)$ for cluster $n$ at centre frequency $f_c$ is:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-1 -->
$$OL_n(f_c) = \frac{\alpha(f_c)}{1000}\cdot\left(d_{3D} + c\cdot(\tau_n + \tau_\Delta)\right)\ \text{[dB]}$$

where $\alpha(f_c)$ is the frequency-dependent oxygen loss in dB/km (Table 7.6.1-1), $c$ is the speed of light in m/s, $d_{3D}$ is the distance in m, $\tau_n$ is the $n$-th cluster delay in s from Step 11, and $\tau_\Delta$ is 0 in the LOS case and $\min(\tau'_n)$ otherwise (the minimum delay in Step 5).

For centre frequencies not listed in the table, $\alpha(f_c)$ is obtained by **linear interpolation** between the two adjacent tabulated centre frequencies.

|---|---|
| 0-52 | 0 |
| 53 | 1 |
| 54 | 2.2 |
| 55 | 4 |
| 56 | 6.6 |
| 57 | 9.7 |
| 58 | 12.6 |
| 59 | 14.6 |
| 60 | 15 |
| 61 | 14.6 |
| 62 | 14.3 |
| 63 | 10.5 |
| 64 | 6.8 |

_The TR prints this table as a two-row strip (frequency header row, loss value row); it is stored here one entry per frequency bin. Machine-readable copies: `oxygen_absorption_loss` in `7.6-additional-components.yaml` and `tables/table-7.6.1-1.csv`._

For large channel bandwidth, the time-domain channel response of each cluster is first transformed to the frequency domain (all rays within one cluster share a common oxygen absorption loss for simplicity), and the loss is applied per frequency $f_c + \Delta f$ within the considered bandwidth:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-2 -->
$$OL_n(f_c + \Delta f) = \frac{\alpha(f_c + \Delta f)}{1000}\cdot\left(d_{3D} + c\cdot(\tau_n + \tau_\Delta)\right)\ \text{[dB]}$$

with $\Delta f \in [-B/2,\ B/2]$ for bandwidth $B$, again linearly interpolated for frequencies not in Table 7.6.1-1. The final frequency-domain response is the sum over all clusters; the time-domain response follows by the reverse transform.

## 7.6.3 Spatial consistency

### 7.6.3.1 Spatial consistency procedure

The spatial consistency procedure makes both cluster-specific and ray-specific random variables **spatially consistent** for drop-based simulations. It can be used on its own or together with Procedure B (§7.6.3.2) for spatially consistent mobility simulations.

It is a **2D random process in the horizontal plane** over UT locations, driven by the parameter-specific correlation distances of Table 7.6.3.1-2. The cluster-specific random variables it covers are: the cluster-specific random delay (Step 5), the cluster-specific shadowing (Step 6), and the cluster-specific offset and sign for AOD/AOA/ZOD/ZOA (Step 7); optionally, for large bandwidth (§7.6.2.2), it may also apply to the parameters of rays within a cluster. The procedure applies to each cluster **before** sorting the delays, and the cluster-specific angular sign is held fixed per drop even as the UT moves.

The ray-specific random variables are: the random coupling of rays (Step 8), the XPR (Step 9), and the random phase (Step 10). The random coupling of rays (Step 8) and the intra-cluster delays (Step 11) are likewise held fixed per drop.

### 7.6.3.2 Spatially-consistent UT/BS mobility modelling

Two alternative procedures are defined. Both consider the downlink direction, as in §7.5.

#### Procedure A

At $t_0 = 0$, when a UT/BS is dropped into the network, spatially consistent cluster powers/delays/angles are generated per §7.6.3.1. The update distance is limited to within 1 m (i.e. $v\cdot\Delta t < 1$ m), taking the closest realization rather than $t_0 = 0$; if the absolute-time-of-arrival model of §7.6.9 is used simultaneously, the update distance is the minimum of 1 m and $d_{3D}/10$. At $t_k = t_{k-1} + \Delta t$, the cluster power/delay/angles are updated from their values at $t_{k-1}$, together with the moving speed, moving direction and UT position.

**Cluster delay** is updated as:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-9 -->
$$\tilde\tau_n(t_k) = \begin{cases} \tilde\tau_n(t_{k-1}) - \dfrac{\hat r_{\text{rx},n}(t_{k-1})^T \bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_{k-1}) + \hat r_{\text{tx},n}(t_{k-1})^T \bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_{k-1})}{c}\,\Delta t & \text{for } k > 0\\[2ex] \tau_n(t_0) + \tau_\Delta(t_0) + \dfrac{d_{3D}(t_0)}{c} & \text{for } k = 0\end{cases}$$

where $c$ is the speed of light in m/s; $\bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_k)$ and $\bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_k)$ are the BS and UT velocity vectors with $\|\bar v_{tx}(t_k)\|_2 = v_{tx}$ and $\|\bar v_{rx}(t_k)\|_2 = v_{rx}$; $d_{3D}(t_0)$ is the 3D Rx–Tx distance at $t_0$ in m; $\tau_n(t_0)$ are the cluster delays in s as in Step 11 of §7.5; and

$$\tau_\Delta(t_0) = \begin{cases}0 & \text{for LOS}\\ \min\left(\{\tau'_n\}_{n=1}^{N}\right) & \text{for NLOS}\end{cases}$$

with $\tau'_n$ the delays from Equation (7.5-1). If the absolute-time-of-arrival modelling of §7.6.9 is used simultaneously, $\tau_\Delta(t_0) = 0$ for LOS and $\Delta\tau$ for NLOS, with $\Delta\tau$ determined per §7.6.9.

Also:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-10 -->
$$\hat r_{\text{rx},n}(t_{k-1}) = \begin{bmatrix}\sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)\cos\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)\sin\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)\\ \cos\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)\end{bmatrix}$$

where $\theta_{n,ZOA}$ and $\phi_{n,AOA}$ are the cluster-specific zenith and azimuth angles of arrival, and

<!-- Eq. 7.6-10aa -->
$$\hat r_{\text{tx},n}(t_{k-1}) = \begin{bmatrix}\sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)\cos\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)\\ \sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)\sin\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)\\ \cos\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)\end{bmatrix}$$

where $\theta_{n,ZOD}$ and $\phi_{n,AOD}$ are the cluster-specific zenith and azimuth angles of departure.

After updating the delays with (7.6-9), the delays over the mobility range are normalized — Equation (7.5-2) of the fast fading model is replaced by:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-10a -->
$$\tau_n(t_k) = \tilde\tau_n(t_k) - \min\left(\left\{\tilde\tau_n(t_k)\right\}_{n=1}^{N}\right)$$

in which $t_k$ covers the entire duration of the mobility model.

**Cluster powers** are updated as in Step 6 using the cluster delays from (7.6-10a).

**Cluster departure angles** ($\theta_{n,ZOD}$, $\phi_{n,AOD}$) **and arrival angles** ($\theta_{n,ZOA}$, $\phi_{n,AOA}$) are updated using a cluster-wise transformation of the UT/BS velocity vector:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-10b -->
$$\bar v'_{n,\text{rx}}(t_k) = \begin{cases}\bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_k) - \bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_k) & \text{for LOS}\\ R_{n,\text{rx}}\cdot\bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_k) - \bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_k) & \text{for NLOS}\end{cases}$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-10c -->
$$\bar v'_{n,\text{tx}}(t_k) = \begin{cases}\bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_k) - \bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_k) & \text{for LOS}\\ R_{n,\text{tx}}\cdot\bar v_{\text{tx}}(t_k) - \bar v_{\text{rx}}(t_k) & \text{for NLOS}\end{cases}$$

with

$$R_{n,\text{rx}} = R_Z\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_k) + \pi\right)\cdot R_Y\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_k)\right)\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & X_n & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\cdot R_Y\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_k)\right)\cdot R_Z\left(-\phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_k)\right)$$

$$R_{n,\text{tx}} = R_Z\left(-\phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_k)\right)\cdot R_Y\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_k)\right)\cdot\begin{bmatrix}1 & 0 & 0\\ 0 & X_n & 0\\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{bmatrix}\cdot R_Y\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2} - \theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_k)\right)\cdot R_Z\left(\phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_k) + \pi\right)$$

where $R_Y(\beta)$ and $R_Z(\alpha)$ are the rotation matrices about the y- and z-axes defined in Equation (7.1-2). The random variable $X_n \in \{1, -1\}$ is sampled from a uniform distribution on an NLOS cluster basis and is not changed during UT mobility within a drop. The cluster-specific decorrelation distances are **60 m, 15 m, 50 m and 10 m** for RMa, UMi, UMa and Indoor scenarios respectively.

The departure and arrival angles in radians are then updated as:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-11 -->
$$\phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_k) = \phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_{k-1}) + \frac{\bar v'_{n,\text{rx}}(t_{k-1})^T\,\hat\phi\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1}),\, \phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)}{c\cdot\tilde\tau_n(t_{k-1})\sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)}\,\Delta t$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-12 -->
$$\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_k) = \theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1}) + \frac{\bar v'_{n,\text{rx}}(t_{k-1})^T\,\hat\theta\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOD}}(t_{k-1}),\, \phi_{n,\text{AOD}}(t_{k-1})\right)}{c\cdot\tilde\tau_n(t_{k-1})}\,\Delta t$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-13 -->
$$\phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_k) = \phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_{k-1}) + \frac{\bar v'_{n,\text{tx}}(t_{k-1})^T\,\hat\phi\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1}),\, \phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)}{c\cdot\tilde\tau_n(t_{k-1})\sin\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)}\,\Delta t$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-14 -->
$$\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_k) = \theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1}) + \frac{\bar v'_{n,\text{tx}}(t_{k-1})^T\,\hat\theta\left(\theta_{n,\text{ZOA}}(t_{k-1}),\, \phi_{n,\text{AOA}}(t_{k-1})\right)}{c\cdot\tilde\tau_n(t_{k-1})}\,\Delta t$$

with $\hat\theta(\theta,\phi)$ and $\hat\phi(\theta,\phi)$ the spherical unit vectors defined in Equations (7.1-13) and (7.1-14).

#### Procedure B

In Procedure B, spatial or time evolution of the channel is obtained by generating channel realizations **separately for all links to different Rx positions**, using Steps 1–12 of §7.5 together with the spatially consistent procedure of §7.6.3.1. Under mobility these positions may be a function of time along one or more Rx trajectories. To keep the spatial/time evolution of delays and angles within reasonable limits, **Steps 5, 6 and 7 of §7.5 are replaced** by the procedure below. (For implementation purposes, LSPs and SSPs may be interpolated within the coherence length or time of the respective parameter.)

**Step 5: Generate cluster delays** $\tau_n$, $n \in [1, N]$. $N$ delays are drawn randomly from a uniform distribution:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-15 -->
$$\tau'_n \sim \text{unif}\left(0,\ 2\cdot 10^{\mu_{\lg\text{DS}} + \sigma_{\lg\text{DS}}}\right)$$

Normalise by subtracting the minimum delay, $\tau_n = \tau'_n - \min(\tau'_n)$. The autocorrelation distance for $\tau'_n$ is $2c\cdot 10^{\mu_{\lg\text{DS}} + \sigma_{\lg\text{DS}}}$. In the LOS case, set the delay of the first cluster $\tau_1$ to 0.

**Step 6: Generate arrival and departure angles** for both azimuth and elevation, using (7.6-16) instead of (7.5-9) and (7.5-14):

<!-- Eq. 7.6-16 -->
$$\phi'_{n,\text{AOA}} \sim 2\cdot 10^{\mu_{\lg\text{ASA}} + \sigma_{\lg\text{ASA}}}\ \text{unif}(-1, 1)$$

with $n \in [1, N]$, repeated independently for AOD, AOA, ZOD and ZOA with the corresponding maximum angles. In the LOS case, set the angles of the first cluster ($\phi'_{1,AOA}$, etc.) to 0. The autocorrelation distances are $2c\cdot 10^{\mu_{\lg\text{DS}} + \sigma_{\lg\text{DS}}}$ for AOD and ZOD, and fixed at 50 m for AOA and ZOA. Offset angles are applied in the modified Step 7b, after cluster powers have been calculated.

**Step 7: Generate cluster powers** $P_n$, assuming a single-slope exponential power profile and Laplacian angular power profiles:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17 -->
$$P'_n = \exp\left(\frac{-\tau'_n}{\text{DS}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{-\sqrt{2}\left|\phi'_{n,\text{AOA}}\right|}{\text{ASA}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{-\sqrt{2}\left|\phi'_{n,\text{AOD}}\right|}{\text{ASD}}\right)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{-\sqrt{2}\left|\theta'_{n,\text{ZOA}}\right|}{\text{ZSA}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{-\sqrt{2}\left|\theta'_{n,\text{ZOD}}\right|}{\text{ZSD}}\right)\cdot 10^{\frac{-Z_n}{10}}$$

where $Z_n \sim N(0, \zeta^2)$ (autocorrelation distance the same as for shadow fading) is the per-cluster shadowing term in dB, and DS, ASA, ASD, ZSA, ZSD are generated in Step 4 of §7.5. Normalize so the cluster powers sum to one:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17a -->
$$P_n = \frac{P'_n}{\sum_{n=1}^{N} P'_n}$$

*In the case of LOS condition*, substitute DS with $\sqrt{1 + K_R/2}\cdot\text{DS}$ and {ASA, ASD, ZSA, ZSD} with {$\sqrt{1+K_R}\cdot\text{ASA}$, $\sqrt{1+K_R}\cdot\text{ASD}$, $\sqrt{1+K_R}\cdot\text{ZSA}$, $\sqrt{1+K_R}\cdot\text{ZSD}$} respectively to preserve the delay and angular spreads, where $K_R$ is the Ricean K-factor from Step 4 converted to linear scale. An additional specular component is added to the first cluster, with power:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17b -->
$$P_{1,LOS} = \frac{K_R}{K_R + 1}$$

and the cluster powers are **not** normalized as in (7.6-17a), but as:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17c -->
$$P_n = \frac{1}{K_R + 1}\frac{P'_n}{\sum_{n=1}^{N} P'_n} + \delta(n-1)\,P_{1,LOS}$$

where $\delta(\cdot)$ is Dirac's delta function. The power of each ray within a cluster is $P_n/M$, with $M$ the number of rays per cluster.

**Step 7b: Apply offset angles.** The ray AOA angles are:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17d -->
$$\phi_{n,m,\text{AOA}} = \phi'_{n,\text{AOA}} + \phi_{LOS,\text{AOA}} + c_{ASA}\,\alpha_m$$

where $\phi_{LOS,AOA}$ is the LOS direction defined in the network layout description (Step 1c). AOD generation follows a similar procedure. The ZOA angles are:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17e -->
$$\theta_{n,m,\text{ZOA}} = \phi'_{n,\text{ZOA}} + \bar\theta_{ZOA} + c_{ZSA}\,\alpha_m$$

where $\bar\theta_{ZOA} = 90°$ if the BS-UT link is O2I and $\bar\theta_{ZOA} = \theta_{LOS,ZOA}$ otherwise. The ZOD angles are:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-17f -->
$$\theta_{n,m,\text{ZOD}} = \phi'_{n,\text{ZOD}} + \theta_{LOS,\text{ZOD}} + \mu_{offset,ZOD} + (3/8)\left(10^{\mu_{\lg\text{ZSD}}}\right)\alpha_m$$

where $\mu_{\lg ZSD}$ is the mean of the ZSD log-normal distribution. (Equations 7.6-17e/f are transcribed as printed: the TR's right-hand sides use $\phi'$ rather than $\theta'$ for the zenith terms.)

Some of the delay and angle spreads and standard deviations used in (7.6-15) and (7.6-16) may be frequency-dependent; for multi-frequency simulations per §7.6.5, the largest value among all simulated frequencies is used in (7.6-15) and (7.6-16) so that cluster and ray delays and angles (but not the powers or the resulting spreads) are the same for all frequencies.

### 7.6.3.3 LOS/NLOS, indoor states and O2I parameters

The **LOS state** is determined per the §7.6.3.1 procedure by comparing a realization of a spatially consistent random variable against the distance-dependent LOS probability: if the realization is less than the LOS probability the state is LOS, otherwise NLOS. This decision is used in Step 2 of §7.5. The same procedure applies to the **indoor state**, using the indoor probability. Correlation distances for both come from Table 7.6.3.1-2.

The **indoor distance** is modelled as the minimum of two spatially consistent uniform random variables within (0, 25) metres, with correlation distance 25 m.

For an indoor UT the pathloss model changes and a penetration loss applies (see §7.4.3). For spatial consistency, the penetration loss deviation $\sigma_P$ — which represents variations within and between buildings of the same type — is modelled as a spatially consistent random variable with correlation distance 10 m. The **building type** is determined by a spatially consistent uniform random variable with correlation distance 50 m, compared against $P_1$, the probability of the low-loss building type: below $P_1$ the building is low loss, otherwise high loss.

Cluster-specific and ray-specific random variables (§7.6.3.1) on the **same floor** are generated in the spatial consistency modelling; across different floors they are uncorrelated.

A transition from LOS to NLOS due to UT mobility causes a hard transition in the channel response, since pathloss and LS parameters differ between the states. The optional **soft LOS state** avoids this by producing a PL and channel impulse response with characteristics of both. $\text{LOS}_{soft}$ is generated as a floating number between 0 (NLOS) and 1 (LOS) in the spatial consistency modelling:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-18 -->
$$\text{LOS}_{soft} = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{\pi}\arctan\left(\sqrt{\frac{20}{\lambda}}\left(G + F(d)\right)\right)$$

where $G$ is a spatially consistent Gaussian random variable with correlation distance per Table 7.6.3.1-2; $F(d) = \sqrt{2}\,\text{erf}^{-1}\left(2\Pr_{\text{LOS}}(d) - 1\right)$; and $\Pr_{\text{LOS}}(d)$ is the distance-dependent LOS probability function.

Once $\text{LOS}_{soft}$ is obtained, Steps 2–12 of §7.5 are performed twice — once with the Step-2 propagation condition set to LOS and once to NLOS — giving $H^{\text{LOS}}$ (LOS pathloss formula and channel model parameters) and $H^{\text{NLOS}}$. The channel matrix with soft LOS state is the linear combination:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-19 -->
$$H\left(\text{LOS}_{soft}\right) = H^{\text{LOS}}\,\text{LOS}_{soft} + H^{\text{NLOS}}\sqrt{1 - \text{LOS}_{soft}^{2}}$$

Soft indoor/outdoor states are **not** modelled in this TR, so the model does not support indoor/outdoor transitions in mobility simulations.

### 7.6.3.4 Applicability of spatial consistency

Two types of spatial consistency are defined across TRPs:

- **Site-specific** — parameters for different BS-UT links are uncorrelated, but the parameters for links from co-sited sectors to a UT are correlated.
- **All-correlated** — BS-UT links are correlated.

### Table 7.6.3.4-1: Correlation type among TRPs

| parameter | correlation_type |
|---|---|
| Delays | Site-specific |
| Cluster powers | Site-specific |

## 7.6.4 Blockage

Blockage modelling is an add-on feature; the method applies only when the feature is turned on, and the temporal variability of the blockage parameters is on-demand. Blockage modelling does **not** change the LOS/NLOS state of a link. When applied, the §7.5 channel generation gains several additional steps between Step 9 and Step 10 (see Figure 7.6.4-1 in TR 38.901 §7.6.4).

Two alternative models are provided. **Model A** is a stochastic method, applicable when generic and computationally efficient blockage modelling is desired. **Model B** is a geometric method, applicable when specific and more realistic blocking modelling is desired.

### 7.6.4.1 Blockage model A

**Step a: Determine the number of blockers.** Multiple 2D angular blocking regions, in terms of centre angle and azimuth/elevation angular span, are generated around the UT. There is one self-blocking region and $K_{block} = 4$ non-self-blocking regions ($K_{block}$ may be changed for certain scenarios, e.g. higher blocker density). The self-blocking component captures human body blocking.

**Step b: Generate the size and location of each blocker.** For self-blocking, the blocking region in UT LCS is defined in terms of elevation and azimuth angles $(\theta'_{sb}, \phi'_{sb})$ and azimuth/elevation angular span $(x_{sb}, y_{sb})$:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-20 -->
$$\left\{(\theta', \phi')\ \middle|\ \left(\theta'_{sb} - \frac{y_{sb}}{2} \le \theta' \le \theta'_{sb} + \frac{y_{sb}}{2},\ \ \phi'_{sb} - \frac{x_{sb}}{2} \le \phi' \le \phi'_{sb} + \frac{x_{sb}}{2}\right)\right\}$$

### Table 7.6.4.1-1: Self-blocking region parameters

| mode | phi_sb_deg | x_sb_deg | theta_sb_deg | y_sb_deg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Portrait mode | 260 | 120 | 100 | 80 |
| Landscape mode | 40 | 160 | 110 | 75 |

_All four columns are angles in degrees; the TR labels them $\phi'_{sb}$, $x_{sb}$, $\theta'_{sb}$, $y_{sb}$._

For non-self-blocking $k = 1, \ldots, 4$, the blocking region in GCS is:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-21 -->
$$\left\{(\theta, \phi)\ \middle|\ \left(\theta_k - \frac{y_k}{2} \le \theta \le \theta_k + \frac{y_k}{2},\ \ \phi_k - \frac{x_k}{2} \le \phi \le \phi_k + \frac{x_k}{2}\right)\right\}$$

### Table 7.6.4.1-2: Blocking region parameters

| scenario | phi_k_deg | x_k_deg | theta_k_deg | y_k_deg | r_m |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| InH scenario | Uniform in [0, 360] | Uniform in [15, 45] | 90 | Uniform in [5, 15] | 2 |
| UMi, UMa, SMa, RMa scenarios | Uniform in [0, 360] | Uniform in [5, 15] | 90 | 5 | 10 |

_Angles in degrees, $r$ (the distance between the UT and the blocker) in metres; blocker index $k = 1, \ldots, 4$._

**Step c: Determine the attenuation of each cluster due to blockers.** The attenuation due to **self-blocking**, corresponding to the centre angle pair $(\theta'_{sb}, \phi'_{sb})$, is **30 dB** provided that $\left|\phi'_{\text{AOA}} - \phi'_{sb}\right| < \frac{x_{sb}}{2}$ and $\left|\theta'_{\text{ZOA}} - \theta'_{sb}\right| < \frac{y_{sb}}{2}$; otherwise it is 0 dB.

The attenuation due to the **non-self-blocking** regions ($k = 1, \ldots, 4$) is:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-22 -->
$$L_{dB} = -20\log_{10}\left(1 - \left(F_{A_1} + F_{A_2}\right)\left(F_{Z_1} + F_{Z_2}\right)\right)$$

provided that $\left|\phi_{\text{AOA}} - \phi_k\right| < x_k$ and $\left|\theta_{\text{ZOA}} - \theta_k\right| < y_k$; otherwise it is 0 dB. The terms are:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-23 -->
$$F_{A_1 | A_2 | Z_1 | Z_2} = \frac{\tan^{-1}\left(\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\,r\left(\frac{1}{\cos\left(A_1 | A_2 | Z_1 | Z_2\right)} - 1\right)}\right)}{\pi}$$

where

<!-- Eq. 7.6-24 -->
$$A_1 = \phi_{\text{AOA}} - \left(\phi_k + \frac{x_k}{2}\right)$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-25 -->
$$A_2 = \phi_{\text{AOA}} - \left(\phi_k - \frac{x_k}{2}\right)$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-26 -->
$$Z_1 = \theta_{\text{ZOA}} - \left(\theta_k + \frac{y_k}{2}\right)$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-27 -->
$$Z_2 = \theta_{\text{ZOA}} - \left(\theta_k - \frac{y_k}{2}\right)$$

In the formula for $F_{A_1|A_2|Z_1|Z_2}$, $\lambda$ is the wavelength and the appropriate signs ($\pm$) within the $\tan^{-1}$ term are given by Table 7.6.4.1-3.

### Table 7.6.4.1-3: Description of signs

| aoa_range | zoa_range | signs_a1_a2 | signs_z1_z2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| x_k/2 < phi_AOA - phi_k <= x_k | -y_k < theta_ZOA - theta_k <= -y_k/2 | (-, +) | (+, -) |
| x_k/2 < phi_AOA - phi_k <= x_k | -y_k/2 < theta_ZOA - theta_k <= y_k/2 | (-, +) | (+, +) |
| x_k/2 < phi_AOA - phi_k <= x_k | y_k/2 < theta_ZOA - theta_k <= y_k | (-, +) | (-, +) |

**Step d: Spatial and temporal consistency of each blocker.** The centre of the blocker is generated from a uniformly distributed random variable that is temporally and spatially consistent. The 2D autocorrelation function $R(\Delta_x, \Delta_t)$ is described with sufficient accuracy by the exponential function:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-28 -->
$$R\left(\Delta_x, \Delta_t\right) = \exp\left(-\left(\frac{\left|\Delta_x\right|}{d_{corr}} + \frac{\left|\Delta_t\right|}{t_{corr}}\right)\right)$$

The spatial correlation distance $d_{corr}$ for the random variable determining the centre of the blocker is given in Table 7.6.4.1-4. The correlation time is $t_{corr} = d_{corr}/v$, where $v$ is the speed of the moving blocker.

### 7.6.4.2 Blockage model B

Model B adopts a geometric method for capturing e.g. human and vehicular blocking.

**Step a: Determine blockers.** A number $K_{block}$ of blockers are modelled as rectangular screens physically placed on the map. Each screen has height $h_k$ and width $w_k$, with the screen centre at coordinate $(x_k, y_k, z_k)$. The number of blockers, their vertical and horizontal extents, locations, density and movement pattern are all simulation assumptions, so different blocking scenarios can be constructed as a study requires. Because the blocking effect diminishes with increasing distance to the blocker, it may be sufficient in implementation to consider only the $K_{block}$ nearest blockers, or those closer than some distance from a specific UT.

### Table 7.6.4.2-5: Recommended blocker parameters

| environment | blocker | dimensions | mobility_pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Indoor; Outdoor; InF | Human | Cartesian: w=0.3m; h=1.7m | Stationary or up to 3 km/h |
| Outdoor | Vehicle | Cartesian: w=4.8m; h=1.4m | Stationary or up to 100 km/h |
| InF | AGV | Cartesian: w=3m; h=1.5m | Up to 30 km/h |
| InF | Industrial robot | Cartesian: w=2m; h=0.2m | Up to 3 m/s |

_The TR numbers this table 7.6.4.2-5 (there is no 7.6.4.2-1..-4 in v19.4.0); the numbering is preserved verbatim._

**Step b: Determine the blockage attenuation per sub-path.** Attenuation caused by each blocker to each sub-path is modelled with a simple knife-edge diffraction model:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-29 -->
$$L_{dB} = -20\log_{10}\left(1 - \left(F_{h_1} + F_{h_2}\right)\left(F_{w_1} + F_{w_2}\right)\right)$$

where $F_{h_1}$, $F_{h_2}$, $F_{w_1}$, $F_{w_2}$ account for knife-edge diffraction at the four edges:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-30 -->
$$F_{h_1 | h_2 | w_1 | w_2} = \begin{cases}\dfrac{\tan^{-1}\left(\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\left(D1_{h_1 | h_2 | w_1 | w_2} + D2_{h_1 | h_2 | w_1 | w_2} - r\right)}\right)}{\pi} & \text{for direct path in LOS}\\[3ex] \dfrac{\tan^{-1}\left(\pm\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\lambda}\left(D1_{h_1 | h_2 | w_1 | w_2} - r'\right)}\right)}{\pi} & \text{for all other paths}\end{cases}$$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength; $D1_{h_1|h_2|w_1|w_2}$ are the projected (onto the side- and top-view planes) distances between the **receiver** and the four edges of the blocker, and $D2_{h_1|h_2|w_1|w_2}$ the projected distances between the **transmitter** and those edges (see Figure 7.6.4.2-2 in TR 38.901 §7.6.4.2). The side-view plane is perpendicular to the horizontal ground plane and the top view perpendicular to the side view. For each cluster the blocker screen is rotated around its centre so the arrival direction of the corresponding path is always perpendicular to the screen (different rotations are required per sub-path), while the base and top edges stay parallel to the horizontal plane. $r$ is the Tx–Rx distance for the direct path in LOS, and $r'$ is the distance between the blocker screen and the receiver projected onto the incoming sub-path direction for all other (NLOS) paths.

The plus/minus signs are determined so that: if the sub-path (terminated at the receiver or transmitter) does **not** intersect the screen in **side view**, the minus sign applies to the shortest path among $D1_{h_1}$ and $D1_{h_2}$ in the NLOS case ($D1_{h_1} + D2_{h_1}$ and $D1_{h_2} + D2_{h_2}$ in the LOS case) and the plus sign to the other edge; if it does not intersect the screen in **top view**, the same rule applies to $D1_{w_1}$/$D1_{w_2}$; and if the sub-path **does** intersect the screen, plus signs apply to both edges. For multiple screens the total loss is the sum of the per-screen losses in dB. Model B is consistent in time, frequency and space, and is more appropriate for simulations with arbitrarily designated blocker density.

## 7.6.8 Explicit ground reflection model

When the ground reflection is to be modelled explicitly, Equation (7.5-30) of §7.5 is replaced by:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-32 -->
$$H^{\text{LOS}}_{u,s}(\tau, t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K_R + 1}}\,H^{\text{NLOS}}_{u,s}\left(\tau - \tau_{\text{LOS}}, t\right) + \sqrt{\frac{K_R}{K_R + 1}}\left(H^{\text{LOS}}_{u,s,1}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_{\text{LOS}}\right) + \frac{d_{3D}}{d_{\text{GR}}}H^{\text{GR}}_{u,s}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_{\text{GR}}\right)\right)$$

with the delays of the ground-reflected and LOS paths defined by their lengths, from the Tx–Rx separation $d_{2D}$ and the Tx and Rx heights $h_{tx}$, $h_{rx}$:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-33 -->
$$\tau_{\text{GR}} = \frac{d_{\text{GR}}}{c} = \frac{\sqrt{\left(h_{tx} + h_{rx}\right)^2 + d_{2D}^{2}}}{c}$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-34 -->
$$\tau_{\text{LOS}} = \frac{d_{3D}}{c} = \frac{\sqrt{\left(h_{tx} - h_{rx}\right)^2 + d_{2D}^{2}}}{c}$$

The channel coefficient for the ground-reflected path is:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-35 -->
$$H^{\text{GR}}_{u,s}(t) = \begin{bmatrix}F_{rx,u,\theta}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOA}}\right)\\ F_{rx,u,\phi}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOA}}\right)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}R^{\text{GR}}_{\parallel} & 0\\ 0 & -R^{\text{GR}}_{\perp}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}F_{tx,s,\theta}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOD}}\right)\\ F_{tx,s,\phi}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOD}}\right)\end{bmatrix}\cdot\exp\left(-j2\pi\frac{d_{\text{GR}}}{\lambda_0}\right)\exp\left(j2\pi\frac{\hat r^{T}_{rx,\text{GR}}\cdot\bar d_{rx,u}}{\lambda_0}\right)\exp\left(j2\pi\frac{\hat r^{T}_{tx,\text{GR}}\cdot\bar d_{tx,s}}{\lambda_0}\right)\exp\left(j2\pi\frac{\hat r^{T}_{rx,\text{GR}}\cdot\bar v}{\lambda_0}t\right)$$

with the normalized vectors pointing towards the ground reflection point from the Tx:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-36 -->
$$\hat r_{tx,\text{GR}} = e_r\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOD}}\right) = \begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\cos\phi_{\text{GR,AOD}}\\ \sin\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\sin\phi_{\text{GR,AOD}}\\ \cos\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\end{bmatrix}$$

and from the Rx side:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-37 -->
$$\hat r_{rx,\text{GR}} = e_r\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}, \phi_{\text{GR,AOA}}\right) = \begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}\cos\phi_{\text{GR,AOA}}\\ \sin\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}\sin\phi_{\text{GR,AOA}}\\ \cos\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}}\end{bmatrix}$$

The angles for the ground-reflected path follow from the geometry, assuming a flat surface whose normal points in the z-direction. At the Tx side:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-37a -->
$$\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}} = 180° - \text{atan}\left(\frac{d_{2D}}{h_{rx} + h_{tx}}\right)$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-37b -->
$$\phi_{\text{GR,AOD}} = \phi_{\text{LOS,AOD}}$$

and at the Rx side:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-37c -->
$$\theta_{\text{GR,ZOA}} = \theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-37d -->
$$\phi_{\text{GR,AOA}} = \phi_{\text{GR,AOD}} + 180°$$

The reflection coefficients for parallel and perpendicular polarization on the ground are:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-38 -->
$$R^{\text{GR}}_{\parallel} = \frac{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0}\cos\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right) + \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0} - \sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right)}}{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0}\cos\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right) - \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0} - \sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right)}}$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-39 -->
$$R^{\text{GR}}_{\perp} = \frac{\cos\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right) + \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0} - \sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right)}}{\cos\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right) - \sqrt{\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0} - \sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\text{GR,ZOD}}\right)}}$$

with the complex relative permittivity of the ground material:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-40 -->
$$\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{GR}}}{\varepsilon_0} = \varepsilon_r - j\,\frac{\sigma}{2\pi f_c \varepsilon_0}$$

The electric constant $\varepsilon_0$ is $8.854187817\ldots \times 10^{-12}$ F·m⁻¹. For applicable frequency ranges, the real relative permittivity and the conductivity in S/m are modelled by:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-41 -->
$$\varepsilon_r = a_\varepsilon\cdot\left(\frac{f_c}{10^{9}}\right)^{b_\varepsilon}$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-42 -->
$$\sigma = c_\sigma\cdot\left(\frac{f_c}{10^{9}}\right)^{d_\sigma}$$

with $f_c$ the centre frequency in Hz.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 7.6.9 Absolute time of arrival

To support simulations in which absolute time of arrival is important, the propagation time delay due to the total path length is considered in Step 11 of the fast fading model. The NLOS impulse response uses (7.6-43) instead of (7.5-27), and the LOS impulse response uses (7.6-44) instead of (7.5-30), where $c$ is the speed of light. The excess delay $\Delta\tau$ is generated from a lognormal distribution with the parameters of Table 7.6.9-1, independently for links between the same UT and different BS sites. For **InF and InH** scenarios the NLOS excess delay is further upper bounded by $2L/c$, where $L$ is the largest dimension of the factory hall / office room, i.e. $L = \max(\text{length}, \text{width}, \text{height})$; for **UMi-Street Canyon, UMa, SMa and RMa** the excess delay is not upper bounded.

<!-- Eq. 7.6-43 -->
$$H^{NLOS}_{u,s}(\tau, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{m \in R_i} H^{NLOS}_{u,s,n,m}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_{n,i} - \frac{d_{3D}}{c} - \Delta\tau\right) + \sum_{n=3}^{N} H^{NLOS}_{u,s,n}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_n - \frac{d_{3D}}{c} - \Delta\tau\right)$$

<!-- Eq. 7.6-44 -->
$$H^{LOS}_{u,s}(\tau, t) = \sqrt{\frac{1}{K_R + 1}}\,H^{NLOS}_{u,s}(\tau, t) + \sqrt{\frac{K_R}{K_R + 1}}\,H^{LOS}_{u,s,1}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_1 - d_{3D}/c\right)$$

where, in the LOS case,

<!-- Eq. 7.6-44a -->
$$H^{NLOS}_{u,s}(\tau, t) = \sum_{n=1}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{m \in R_i} H^{NLOS}_{u,s,n,m}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_{n,i} - \frac{d_{3D}}{c}\right) + \sum_{n=3}^{N} H^{NLOS}_{u,s,n}(t)\,\delta\left(\tau - \tau_n - \frac{d_{3D}}{c}\right)$$

(Equation 7.6-43 is printed in the TR as "(7.6.-43)" — an evident typographical slip for (7.6-43); the canonical dash form is used here.)

## 7.6.10 Dual mobility

To support simulations involving dual Tx and Rx mobility or scatterer mobility, the Doppler frequency component in the channel coefficient generation of Step 11 in §7.5 is updated as follows. For the **LOS path**:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-45 -->
$$v_{n,m} = \frac{\hat r^{T}_{rx,n,m}\cdot\bar v_{rx} + \hat r^{T}_{tx,n,m}\cdot\bar v_{tx}}{\lambda_0}$$

where $\hat r_{rx,n,m}$ and $\hat r_{tx,n,m}$ are defined in (7.5-23) and (7.5-24) respectively, and

$$\bar v_{rx} = v_{rx}\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{v,rx}\cos\phi_{v,rx} & \sin\theta_{v,rx}\sin\phi_{v,rx} & \cos\theta_{v,rx}\end{bmatrix}^{T}$$

$$\bar v_{tx} = v_{tx}\begin{bmatrix}\sin\theta_{v,tx}\cos\phi_{v,tx} & \sin\theta_{v,tx}\sin\phi_{v,tx} & \cos\theta_{v,tx}\end{bmatrix}^{T}$$

For **all other paths**:

<!-- Eq. 7.6-46 -->
$$v_{n,m} = \frac{\hat r^{T}_{rx,n,m}\cdot\bar v_{rx} + \hat r^{T}_{tx,n,m}\cdot\bar v_{tx} + 2\alpha_{n,m}D_{n,m}}{\lambda_0}$$

where $D_{n,m}$ is a random variable from $-v_{scatt}$ to $v_{scatt}$, $\alpha_{n,m}$ is a Bernoulli random variable with mean $p$, and $v_{scatt}$ is the maximum speed of the clutter. The parameter $p$ determines the proportion of mobile scatterers and can be selected to model statistically larger ($p$ higher) or smaller numbers of mobile scatterers — a completely static environment is $p = 0$, giving all scatterers zero speed. A typical value of $p$ is **0.2**.
